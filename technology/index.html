Technology overview

Because our first system is still in development, this page gives a high-level overview only. The system's architecture, methods, and performance data are proprietary and are not published.

This reflects an active prototype program. Features and capabilities may change and should not be read as final commercial specifications.

Real-time crew awareness

Continuous, real-time awareness of crew status aboard the vessel.

Faster recognition

Helping crews recognize a potential man-overboard event sooner, for faster and more informed response.

Built for working vessels

Grounded in the realities of the deck: motion, weather, noise, demanding work, and limited attention.

Operator-focused design

Clear status information and alerts for the people responsible for the vessel and crew.

Onboard operation

Designed to work aboard the vessel without depending on shore-based connectivity.

Adaptable to the vessel

Configured to each vessel's layout and operations. Final commercial hardware has not yet been selected.

Safety and reliability goals

Consistent detection, manageable false-alert rates, clear operator feedback, local operation without shore connectivity, and dependable function in real vessel conditions. These are engineering goals, not verified commercial performance claims.

How we test

Development has included controlled bench testing, an initial vessel field test, a formal controlled validation session, and an independently validated improvement in performance. Each stage refines the system ahead of expanded vessel testing.

What comes next

Commercial hardware design, alarm integration, vessel-specific configuration, search-and-rescue support, and testing on larger and more complex vessels.

Discuss collaboration

Developing practical marine safety, crew-location, and search-and-rescue technologies for commercial and recreational vessels.
